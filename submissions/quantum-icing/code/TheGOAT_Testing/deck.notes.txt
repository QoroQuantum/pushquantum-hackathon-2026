--- cover ---
One night of measurements on network24, the largest instance. The quantum pieces work and scale, and always produce valid topologies. On the block subproblems they do not beat classical simulated annealing on the same model, which matches what the rest of the team found independently with their own QAOA search. The hand-in value 657,121 survived every attack we made on it.

--- surrogate ---
Before spending any quantum budget we checked the model. For blocks of 6 and 7 freed arcs we enumerated every valid rewiring and scored each with the exact LP referee. The sub-QUBO the earlier quantum LNS used ranks them with a Spearman between 0.33 and 0.67, which is too weak to act on. Plain demand-weighted hop count reaches 0.85 to 0.89. The same enumeration also showed that none of those blocks contains anything better than the hand-in.

--- valid ---
The earlier attempts lost most samples to infeasibility: PCE gave 0 valid out of 100. Here every proposal is valid. A block frees r arcs and only permutes their heads, so degrees cannot break. PCE bitstrings go through the Hungarian algorithm to the nearest permutation. The QAOA variant starts from the incumbent and mixes with exchanges between freed arcs, so the circuit never leaves the permutation space: all raw samples were valid, locally at 16 qubits and on Qoro’s GPU cluster at 25.

--- headtohead ---
Every block gets the same number of exact LP solves for every proposer. The quantum proposer clearly carries information: it beats random rewirings every time and beats the best of 1,500 hop-ranked random samples in most blocks. But classical simulated annealing on the very same learned QUBO finds better candidates in 55 of 60 blocks. Starting a local search from quantum candidates ends where starting from random ones ends. Changing the classical optimizer of the circuit, including quantum natural gradient and QN-SPSA, does not close the gap; inheriting angles from the previous block halves the distance to the QUBO optimum, but the better QUBO energy does not turn into a better LP value.

--- robust ---
Two bounds meet on this slide. From above: degrees pin the neighbourhood down exactly, because if two valid topologies differ in k arcs the added arcs carry exactly the removed arcs’ tails and heads, so the move is a permutation of those k heads with no fixed point. That makes each radius finite and enumerable — 1,128 moves at radius 2, 34,592 at radius 3 — and we enumerated all of them against a warm dual simplex bounded at the incumbent, which refuted every one in six minutes. Within three arcs this is not “we looked and found nothing”, it is “nothing exists”. The same machinery run as a descent improved two other hand-ins, network22 to 604,540 and network18 to 472,112, which a radius-four sweep over 530,145 moves then certified. A kick of five arcs and another exhaustive descent took network18 further, to 469,000, −0.92% against best known — a topology ten arcs away from the one the certificate had proved locally optimal. Both results are true, and together they say exactly what a certificate buys: it bounds a neighbourhood, not an instance, and the only way past it is to leave. From below: every unit of demand crosses at least its shortest-path distance, out-degree two caps how near the graph can be — at most two nodes at distance one, four more at two, eight at three — and the maximum arc load is at least the average, which together put 511,229 under every topology that exists. So the room left under 657,121 is at most 28.5%, and most of that is the bound’s own slack: against the six instances where QOBLIB proves the optimum the same bound lands 1.5 to 18 percent low. Past radius three we are back to searching, and the table lists every attack we made in one night.

--- takeaways ---
Three things worked and are worth reusing. First, measuring whether a surrogate ranks solutions like the referee before spending quantum budget on it. Second, building the constraints into the blocks, the decoder and the mixer, so that every sample is a valid topology even at 25 qubits. Third, always running a classical twin on the same blocks with the same LP budget, which is how we know that on these subproblems simulated annealing is the better optimizer. The next step is the model: the hand-in’s maximum load is 6.7% above the average-load bound, so the room left is in balancing the load, which the hop count does not see.
